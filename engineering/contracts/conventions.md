## 1. Scope

The rules every seam obeys. Each sibling contract cites this document and states only its seam-specific behaviour and specializations; none restates a rule below, and a reviewer treats a convention as carried by citation. A seam may *tighten* a convention (a narrower bound, an extra guarantee) in its own document; it may never loosen one.

## 2. Conventions

- **Closed sets, exhaustively mapped**: where a contract defines a closed set — states, kinds, classifications, causes — every consumer maps every member; a consumer meeting an unmapped member, or a rule meeting a member it does not classify, is a defect by definition.
- **Structured error arms everywhere**: every fallible operation has an explicit error arm. Errors are structured, cause-naming per R10, and say whether retrying can help. No operation ever answers a fault with undefined behaviour, a coerced value from its success domain, or a silent no-op.
- **Fault-class versus validity**: a fault of the system — a store, an index, a derivative pipeline, a credential — is a distinct error class from an input's invalidity, never conflated: the remedies differ (report or retry a fault; fix the input). Every fallible path — intakes, writes, and reads alike — keeps the two classes distinguishable.
- **Unknown and deleted sessions**: every session-scoped operation given an unknown or deleted session draws the structured unknown-session error, with two sanctioned exception classes, each instance declared in its contract: deletion-cascade cleanup steps (they exist to act on deleted sessions and are idempotent instead), and existence queries — an operation whose declared domain is a session's existence answers its question with a definite absent rather than erroring about it. Deletion is observable at its commit: from that point no operation serves the session, nothing writes or emits for it, the cleanup cascade's intermediates are unobservable, and an operation racing the deletion either lands wholly before it — its residue then removed by the cascade — or draws the rejection. One declared third outcome exists: where a seam's access is closed by a cascade step (a credential revocation), calls issued through the not-yet-revoked path serve at worst the *whole* pre-deletion view for the bounded interval until that step lands — never a partial one; each such cutover window is declared by its contract and bounded by the cascade's re-drive.
- **Bounded resolution**: every operation — reads, writes, intakes, evaluations, external calls, and the opening of any connection or subscription — resolves to a result or a structured error in bounded time. Nothing hangs, spins, or waits silently; a stall becomes the structured timeout-class error within its bound.
- **Crash atomicity and re-drive**: every committed state change is observably all-or-nothing — after a crash at any point, a restart observes the prior state or the completed change, with the change's data, events, and transcript notes landing together or not at all, plus any *visible recovery artifacts* the seam declares (an interrupted-classification failure entry, a restored pending message): recovery may add honest markers of the interruption, never a half-visible intermediate of the change itself. Interrupted or live-faulted multi-step work re-drives to completion exactly once — at startup after a crash, and by the owning module's bounded re-attempt while the service runs, so committed-but-uncleaned work never stands indefinitely; re-drive steps are idempotent, and a re-drive never overwrites state a later-committed operation established.
- **Per-entity serialization**: state changes are serialized per entity (a session, the printer-knowledge singleton, a printer). Concurrent operations land whole in some order — the final state is one writer's entire outcome, never a blend — with events in commit order.
- **Emission discipline**: exactly the observable changes emit. Every change to served state or annotations emits its durable event (keyed and state-bearing per the events contract) and, where the seam defines one, its transcript note; an operation that changes nothing observable emits nothing and notes nothing. A contract may declare a bounded-staleness exception explicitly — the events contract's turn-in-flight flag, corrected within a stated bound by the turn's own signals, is one; undeclared exceptions do not exist.
- **Live persistence faults**: a fault that prevents an operation's commit while the service runs is that operation's error arm — the operation fails whole, never completing partially or silently. The exception class is *held-visibly-and-re-driven state*: where failing whole would destroy accepted content or servable knowledge, a contract may declare the fault a visible condition instead — the declared instances are the message-lifecycle contract's persistence-failure condition and the printer-state contract's freshness causes over the retained list; undeclared instances do not exist.

## 3. Test conventions

- **Every normative claim has an obligation**: a behaviour a contract states in prose is asserted by some obligation — a claim with no covering test is a defect of the contract, not implementation freedom.
- **Per operation family, the standard injections**: an injected fault (the structured error observed — cause-naming and saying whether retry can help — with nothing committed by the failed call), an injected stall (resolution within the bound, never an indefinite wait), an injected race where concurrency is reachable (serialization observed, no blend, no loss), and a restart (state and pending work survive per the seam's rules); event-driven and multi-step work additionally takes an injected *live* fault, asserting the owner's bounded re-attempt completes it without a restart — the second half of the re-drive convention.
- **Unknown-session per operation**: the unknown-session error asserted for every operation on the seam — §2's exception classes excepted: cleanup steps asserted completing cleanly, existence queries asserted answering definitely — including after a deletion committed on another device, and at a crash point mid-cascade.
- **Distinctness asserted, not assumed**: where a contract defines two errors or two rejection reasons as distinct, an obligation asserts they are distinguishable to the consumer.
- **Release items**: every external fact the harvest's release-verification list assigns to a seam is carried in that seam's contract as a "Release items (live suite)" scenario before the harvest is deleted — the mocked suites cannot prove these facts, and the release suite asserts them.
- **Obligations are a matrix plus scenarios**: each contract's test-obligations section is a table for the standard axes — rows are the seam's operations and event-driven behaviours, columns the standard checks (fault, stall, race, restart, unknown-session, error-distinctness) — whose cells are terse assertions or an explicit "n/a" with its reason; an empty cell is an undecided cell and a defect of the contract. An axis inapplicable to the *whole* seam may be dropped or renamed to its seam-shaped analogue, with the reason stated above the table; an axis inapplicable only to some rows keeps its column with per-cell "n/a". What is not cell-shaped — multi-step flows, cross-operation races, compound crash cases — follows as a numbered scenario list. Behaviour is specified in the contract's prose sections; the matrix asserts coverage and never redefines behaviour.

## 4. Vocabulary

One owner per shared term; every other document cites rather than redefines.

- **Entity key, entry key, sync token, durable/ephemeral event**: owned by the events contract. The **session-failure condition**'s semantics (aggregation, orthogonality to sends, clearing) are the message-lifecycle contract's; its wire shape and fetchability are the events contract's. The per-turn **message key** is owned by the message-lifecycle contract; a turn's committed entries carry per-entry keys, and the two are never interchangeable.
- **Failure classifications** (auth, no-credentials, usage-limit, outage, protocol, timeout, backend-unavailable, interrupted, internal fault, malformed-output) and the **synchronous/asynchronous line**: owned by the message-lifecycle contract.
- **EvidenceRef, kind classes (image-class / file-class), unknown-ref, read-failure**: owned by the evidence contract.
- **Freshness states and causes, reachability states and causes, the unknown-printer error, telemetry provenance (source, timestamp, fresh window)**: owned by the printer-state contract. **Evidence provenance** — origin metadata: a snapshot's camera and capture time, an upload's filename and upload time, never a fresh window — is the evidence contract's distinct term; the two share a word, not a definition.
- **Binding states and annotations**: owned by the binding contract. The **staleness judgment** on fresh-window-bearing values is the printer-state contract's.
